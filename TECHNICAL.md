# Technical document — The goats

**More than a Hack 2027, Phase 1: Ball Possession**

## Approach

Per-frame labelling (`H` / `A` / `D` / `X`) is treated as a **segmentation** problem, because the
ranking metric (`spellF1`) grades whole possessions, not frames. The pipeline has two layers:

1. **Features** (`solution/preprocessing.ipynb`). One streaming pass over the tactical ATD writes a
   per-frame table: ball position, distance from the ball to the nearest home and nearest away
   player, and the Smart Tagging intervals. No labels, no model.
2. **Decisions** (`solution/*.py`). Two questions, answered in order, each with a Viterbi decoder:
   *is the ball in play?* (`deadball.py`) and, where it is, *which team has it?* (`teams.py`).

The single most important design choice: **a missing ball is not a dead ball.** The baseline writes
`D` whenever the tracker loses the ball, which is 41 % of graded frames and mostly *during* play.
Every spurious `D` splits a real possession in two and `spellF1` loses both halves. Here `D` needs
positive evidence of a stoppage.

### Is the ball in play? (`deadball.py`)

Per frame, a log-odds score for "dead" is the sum of:

| evidence | weight | why |
|---|---|---|
| ball travelling > 2 m in 1 s, inside the pitch | −2.0 | the strongest sign of play: slower than any pass, faster than tracker jitter on a parked ball |
| ball seen beyond a line by > 0.5 m | +3.0 | out is out |
| collective player speed vs the half's median (2 s rolling median) | ±0.5 | weak: slow possession play is as slow as a stoppage (see *Diagnostics*) |
| inside a Smart Tagging `SET PIECES` window, before the detected kick | +1.5 | a restart ends a stoppage |
| first second after the detected kick | −1.5 | play has restarted |
| before the `Kick Off (Start)` of a half | forced | no play before kick-off |
| prior | −0.1 | the ball is in play unless something says otherwise |

The kick is located on the ball track: the first frame in the window where the ball moves faster
than 3 m/s for 3 consecutive frames (found in 58 of the 59 windows). A two-state Viterbi with a cost
of 20 for entering or leaving a stoppage (≈ 1.6 s of full speed evidence) turns the scores into
intervals.

### Which team has it? (`teams.py`)

Per frame with the ball visible, evidence for home (positive) or away (negative):

    evidence = exp(-d_min / 1.5 m) · tanh((d_away - d_home) / 1.0 m)

strong only when a player is actually on the ball; a ball in flight far from everyone says almost
nothing. Inside each in-play interval a two-state Viterbi with a **switch cost of 10** (≈ 0.5 s of
clear control by the other team) decides `H` / `A`: deflections and one-touch duels do not pay for
two switches, a real interception does. Frames without the ball carry no evidence and simply
continue the current possession.

Each change of possession is then **moved to the new team's first touch** after the old team's
last one. Between the two the ball is loose or in the air and Viterbi is indifferent; the ground
truth keeps a loose ball with the last team, and one second late costs a third of `spellF1`.

### Team speed (`team_speed.py`)

The only signal not in the feature table: mean speed of each team's tracked players per frame,
read once from the raw ATD (speeds clipped to 12 m/s) and cached.

## Assets used

| asset | use |
|---|---|
| tactical ATD tracking (players + ball) | ball position, nearest player per team, player speeds |
| automatic Smart Tagging | `SET PIECES` windows (restart detection) and `Kick Off (Start)` |
| half boundaries (`games.half_video_blocks`, published in DATA.md) | frames outside the halves are `X` |
| other Smart Tagging codes | in the feature table, **not used** by the decision layer |
| homography, both videos, broadcast tracking | **not used**: the tracking already gives pitch coordinates |

## Training data

**None.** No model is trained and no labelled data of any kind was used, from this game or any
other. The unit tests use synthetic matches generated by code.

## Parameters and how they were set

Every number lives in `solution/params.py` with its reason next to it. They were **set from what a
football match looks like and from their units** (a pass travels more than 2 m in a second; half a
second of clear control is "controlled possession"; a player within 1.5 m can be playing the
ball), **not by scoring variants on the public window**. RULES.md forbids tuning on it, and
EVALUATION.md shows that doing so picks the wrong answer even for one parameter.

### Diagnostics, without ground truth

`solution/diagnose.py` checks the dead-ball layer on the full match using aggregates only. It is
how the one design change during development was made:

- The first version split "slow players = dead" from "fast = alive" with an Otsu threshold on the
  collective speed. On the full match it called **70 %** of the graded frames dead.
- The diagnostics showed why: the speed distribution is a **single skewed hump**, not two
  populations, so Otsu cut through the middle of play; and the ball was **moving inside the pitch
  in 26–29 % of the "dead" frames**, which a real stoppage almost never shows (slow build-up play).
- Fix: ball motion became the main sign of play and speed weak evidence relative to the median.

For transparency: the public window's confusion matrix of the first version also showed the
over-prediction of `D`. The fix was designed and confirmed on the full-match diagnostics above; no
parameter value was chosen by comparing public-window scores.

## Possession rules implemented

- Possession goes to the team whose player is closest to the ball, weighted by how close; no
  tracklet identities are used (the 46 "Track" slots are fragments, not persons).
- A loose ball, a pass in flight, a deflection or a lost duel does not change possession; about
  half a second of clear control by the other team does.
- A change of possession happens at the new team's first touch.
- Restarts: the ball is dead until the restart kick is detected; possession after a restart goes
  to whichever team then controls the ball.
- Dead ball is never assigned to a team.
- `X` is written outside the two halves and on in-play stretches where the ball is never seen
  (0.4 % of graded frames), which the scorer fills with the previous label.

## Limitations

- When the ball is not tracked, the dead/alive call rests on player speed and on context, the
  weakest signals; stoppages that start and end unseen can be shortened or missed.
- The Smart Tagging has only 59 set pieces (5 free kicks, every throw-in without a team), far fewer
  than the restarts in a match, so many restarts get no kick detection.
- Nearest-player is a poor proxy in duels, crowded boxes and under long balls.
- On the public window we predict 13 possessions against 17: some are merged, probably across short
  stoppages or quick turnovers.
- The parameters are reasoned, not validated on labelled data from other games.
- A goalkeeper holding the ball with slow players around can look like a stoppage.

With more time: the Smart Tagging transition codes as turnover cues, interpolating short ball
gaps, a classifier trained on automatic pseudo-labels for long gaps, and calibration on labelled
data from other games.

## Reproduction

Python 3.12.10. The match data unpacked so that `data/tactical/BAR-ATH_tactical_atd.txt` exists
(or `MTAH_DATA_DIR` pointing at the folder that contains `tactical/`).

```bash
pip install -r requirements-lock.txt
python -m jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute solution/preprocessing.ipynb --output-dir data/processed
python solution/run.py --name "The goats"     # writes evaluation/results/The goats.csv and validates it
python solution/diagnose.py                   # optional: the dead-ball diagnostics above
python -m unittest discover -s solution/tests -t .   # 38 tests
```

The executed notebook and the feature table go to `data/processed/`, which git ignores, so no match
data ends up in the repository. The pipeline has no randomness: the same data gives the same file.
